CONCERNING THE DESIGNATION OF STATE HIGHWAY 86
THROUGH ELBERT COUNTY AS THE PLAINS-TO-PINES SCENIC
CORRIDOR.
WHEREAS, Featuring an average altitude of just under 6,000
feet-several hundred feet higher than that of the Mile High City-State
Highway 86 through Elbert County offers Colorado residents, workers,
westbound travelers, and tourists unrivaled views of the east-facing
Rocky Mountains range, including:
! The magnificent Sangre De Cristo subrange in Southern Colorado;
! The iconic Pikes Peak-elevation 14,115 feet-on which Katharine
Lee Bates composed the lyrics to "America the Beautiful";
! Mount Blue Sky-elevation 14,268 feet-which straddles the
Arapaho and Pike National Forests; and
! Longs Peak-elevation 14,259 feet-located prominently in the
Rocky Mountain National Park Wilderness of Northern Colorado;
and
WHEREAS, The spectacular views of the Rocky Mountain Front

Range from State Highway 86 through Colorado's Eastern Plains, in all
their rural, pastoral splendor, are worthy of state recognition; and
WHEREAS, State Highway 86 is the scenic high-plains thoroughfare
between the Town of Limon on the east and the Town of Castle Rock on
the west, with approximately 61 miles lying within Elbert County; and
WHEREAS, State Highway 86 is the functional main street through
Elbert County's largest municipality, the Town of Elizabeth, which is also
recognized as an official Department of Local Affairs (DOLA) Colorado
Main Street community; and
WHEREAS, Elizabeth is home to over 3,000 people, multiple rustic,
treasure-laden antique shops, the iconic Rambler Ranch, and the lovely
Carriage Shoppes Tea Room located in a historic livery stable that serves
as a cherished regional destination-a pastoral gathering place where
women and their daughters, grandchildren, and out-of-town guests gather;
and
WHEREAS, For the over 700 residents of the historic Town of
Kiowa, another DOLA Colorado Main Street community, State Highway
86 also serves as the functional main street; and
WHEREAS, Kiowa is the official seat of Elbert County government
and home of the Elbert County Historical Society and Museum, which
occupies the historic Kiowa High School building built in 1921; and
WHEREAS, The Elbert County Historical Society and Museum's
Spring 2018 issue of the "Smoky Hill Express" newsletter features a
cover story summarizing the history of the Smoky Hill Trail and evolution
of transportation in Elbert County, including the development of State
Highway 86; and
WHEREAS, State Highway 86 is the primary access route for various
tourist attractions in the Elbert County region, including abundant outdoor
recreation and historically significant sites like Buffalo Groves, Inc., the
Bijou Basin open space, Castlewood Canyon State Park, the Elbert
County Fair-this year celebrating 92 years of cherished livestock and
agricultural traditions and wholesome 4-H opportunities for young
people-and a bevy of small, family-owned businesses that generate

essential tax revenues that benefit the people of Colorado; and
WHEREAS, The vast rolling hills and prairies of the eastern portion
of two-lane State Highway 86 through Colorado's high-plains desert offer
travelers serenely beautiful and unique vistas-frequently including falcon,
hawk, eagle, pronghorn, and deer sightings-that serve as a pastoral
alternative to the hullabaloo of four-lane I-70 and its heavy-duty truck
traffic; and
WHEREAS, In the centuries before European settlers arrived, the
Arapaho, Cheyenne, Kiowa, Ute, Comanche, and other First Nations
peoples of the Great Plains were the first to break in barely discernible
trails that later served as the basis for primitive wagon routes, like that of
the fabled South Branch of the historic Smoky Hill Trail, segments of
which later became the alignment for the landmark Leavenworth and
Pikes Peak Express stagecoach routes; and
WHEREAS, Prior to Colorado becoming a territory in 1861-and long
before achieving statehood in 1876, when much of what is now Colorado
was still part of the Kansas Territory-the 1858 Pikes Peak Gold Rush led
to a massive, four-year migration of prospectors, adventurists, and, as
prominent historian/professor/author Tom Noel described them: "gold
grubbers, town puffers, prostitutes, gamblers, and journalists," who
traveled on foot, on horseback, or by wagon or stagecoach through
Missouri and Kansas toward the Pikes Peak area of the Rocky Mountains
via the storied South Branch of the Smoky Hill Trail, large segments of
which are State Highway 86 today; and
WHEREAS, The Elbert County Board of County Commissioners and
the Mayors and Trustees of the Towns of Kiowa and Elizabeth support
designating State Highway 86 as Colorado's "Plains-to-Pines Scenic
Corridor"; now, therefore,
Be It Resolved by the House of Representatives of the Seventy-fifth
General Assembly of the State of Colorado, the Senate concurring herein:
(1) That the entire portion of State Highway 86 through Elbert
County, Colorado, be officially designated and branded the
"Plains-to-Pines Scenic Corridor";

(2) That the Colorado Department of Transportation (CDOT) is
authorized to accept and expend gifts, grants, and donations for the
purpose of placing initial markers, signage, and xeriscape landscaping
along portions of the route designated as state highway to aesthetically
brand and establish the "Plains-to-Pines Scenic Corridor"; and
(3) That CDOT is authorized to enter into one or more
intergovernmental agreements with the Board of County Commissioners
of Elbert County, the Town of Kiowa, and the Town of Elizabeth for
purposes of maintaining said markers, signage, and associated xeric
vegetation for the "Plains-to-Pines Scenic Corridor".

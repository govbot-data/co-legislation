CONCERNING CELEBRATING FARM WORKERS DAY.
WHEREAS, Farm workers have long been the backbone of American
agriculture; and
WHEREAS, Colorado agriculture generates approximately $47 billion
in annual economic activity and employs between 20,000 and 40,000
workers; and
WHEREAS, Farm workers constitute 4.1% of the total workforce in
rural Colorado; and
WHEREAS, In Colorado, women make up approximately 25% to
30% of the farm worker workforce, mirroring national trends, but with
important local context-roughly 8,000 to 12,000 women are working in
our fields, dairies, and packing operations, and making meaningful
contributions to their local communities and to our state; and
WHEREAS, Many of these female farm workers are Latina and
immigrant women, supporting families both here and across borders
while often being paid less, given fewer protections, and facing higher
risks of harassment and violence; and

WHEREAS, Farm workers are at the core of the Colorado economy,
with multiple industries relying on farm products to supply food to
everyone from local consumers to international markets; and
WHEREAS, Colorado farm workers now enjoy a range of rights, but
many people battled to secure those rights as they exist today. Beginning
in the 1960s, the hard work of attaining these rights was pioneered by the
National Farm Workers Association and the Agricultural Workers
Organizing Committee, which eventually merged to become the United
Farm Workers (UFW); and
WHEREAS, By 1970, the UFW had persuaded grape growers to
accept union contracts and had successfully organized workers across the
agricultural industry; and
WHEREAS, In 1975, the UFW's efforts resulted in the California
"Agricultural Labor Relations Act", a groundbreaking law protecting the
right of farm workers to unionize; and
WHEREAS, Workers at the UFW tirelessly devoted themselves to
making people aware of the struggles of farm workers and their need for
better pay and safer working conditions; and
WHEREAS, Celebrating farm workers is celebrating who we are,
what we eat, and the tireless work of the thousands of Coloradans whose
efforts make our food culture possible; now, therefore,
Be It Resolved by the House of Representatives of the Seventy-fifth
General Assembly of the State of Colorado:
That we, the members of the House of Representatives, recognize
March 31, 2026, as Farm Workers Day and honor the hard work, safety,
and dignity of Colorado's farm workers.

CONCERNING RECOGNIZING THE WEEK OF SEPTEMBER 20
THROUGH SEPTEMBER 26, 2026, AS "FRONTOTEMPORAL
DEGENERATION AWARENESS WEEK".
WHEREAS, The Association for Frontotemporal Degeneration
reports that Frontotemporal Degeneration (FTD) is a terminal and
incurable neurodegenerative disease affecting the frontal and temporal
lobes of the brain; and
WHEREAS, It is the custom of the General Assembly to recognize
official time periods to increase awareness of serious health conditions
that affect the lives of Colorado citizens; and
WHEREAS, In conjunction with the observance of World FTD
Awareness Week, Colorado will join many others in recognizing
September 20 through September 26, 2026, as "Frontotemporal
Degeneration Awareness Week"; and
WHEREAS, FTD causes impairments to speech, personality,

behavior, and motor skills--which constitute a major public health
concern; and
WHEREAS, It takes an average of 3.6 years from the start of
symptoms to get an accurate diagnosis of FTD, with a remaining life
expectancy of 7 to 13 years, on average, after the start of symptoms; and
WHEREAS, FTD strikes people between the ages of 21 and 80, with
the largest percentage of those affected being between 45 and 64,
rendering people in their prime of life unable to work or function
normally; and
WHEREAS, FTD imposes additional average annual costs of care,
impacting those living with the disease; this financial cost is at least 120
percent the economic burden of Alzheimer's disease; and
WHEREAS, FTD is named in The National Plan to Address
Alzheimer's Disease as a related dementia and included as a priority in the
goals and strategies of the Plan to achieve the vision of a nation without
Alzheimer's disease and all other forms of dementia; and
WHEREAS, While FTD is recognized as a related dementia within
the "Talk About Dementia" awareness campaign spearheaded by the
Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment, this resolution
serves to establish "FTD Awareness Week" by supporting the goals laid
out in similar statewide initiatives to comprehensively address and
acknowledge all forms of dementia; and
WHEREAS, With FTD comprising an estimated 5 to 15 percent of all
forms of dementia cases, in aggregate, FTD is the most common form of
dementia for people under 60 years old; and
WHEREAS, Approximately 40 percent of people with FTD have a
family history of FTD or a closely related condition such as ALS
(Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis), and roughly half of those people are
found to have an inherited form manifested in gene mutations in the
Progranulin, C9orf72, Tau/MAPT, and other rarer genes; and
WHEREAS, FTD is often misdiagnosed as either a psychiatric
disorder or a catch-all neurodegenerative disease due to the vast range

and diverse combinations of cognitive and behavioral symptoms, and
most cases of FTD are diagnosed in people aged 45 to 65--an age group
much younger than all other common forms of dementia that typically
affect those over 65; and
WHEREAS, FTD often negatively changes a person's ability to
express emotions and show affection and empathy for loved ones; and
WHEREAS, In the behavioral variant of FTD, a person's sense of
social etiquette and appropriate behavior can be completely lost; it can
also result in a distortion of personality so profound that loved ones may
feel they are living with a complete stranger; and
WHEREAS, In the language variants of FTD, in addition to Primary
Progressive Aphasia (PPA), variants include Semantic Variant PPA
(svPPA, also called Semantic Dementia), Nonfluent/Agrammatic Variant
PPA (nfvPPA), Logopenic Variant PPA (lvPPA), Behavioral Variant
FTD (bvFTD), and FTD with Motor Neuron Disease
(FTD-ALS/MND)--noting the 3 PPA subtypes are distinct, but all are
considered part of the "language-led" (aphasic) spectrum of FTD; and
WHEREAS, Additionally in the language variants of FTD, a person
may experience difficulty speaking and understanding grammar, forget
the meaning of words, and demonstrate a drastically diminished
vocabulary--becoming increasingly hesitant to speak and potentially
losing the ability to speak altogether; and
WHEREAS, In the movement variants of FTD, a person may
experience muscle weakness, falling, loss of balance, difficulty making
speech, difficulty swallowing, or choking; and
WHEREAS, A formal global epidemiology study of FTD is yet to take
place. However, the current estimation is believed to be over 60,000
people affected in the United States, which is likely a paltry fraction of
the total figure, if accounting for the gap of cases that comprise the initial
4 years of misdiagnosis and underdiagnosis in aggregate; and
WHEREAS, The Association for Frontotemporal Degeneration is the
leading national organization exclusively focused on the spectrum of FTD
disorders with a mission to improve the quality of life of people affected

by FTD and drive research to a cure; and
WHEREAS, Raising greater awareness of this serious disease is
imperative, and further action is needed to increase engagement and
advocacy at the local, state, and national levels; now, therefore,
Be It Resolved by the Senate of the Seventy-fifth General Assembly of
the State of Colorado, the House of Representatives concurring herein:
That we, the members of the Colorado General Assembly, proclaim
the week of September 20 through September 26, 2026, as
"Frontotemporal Degeneration Awareness Week" in Colorado.

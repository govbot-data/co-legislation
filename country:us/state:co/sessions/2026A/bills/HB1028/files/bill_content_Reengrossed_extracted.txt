Bill Summary
(Note: This summary applies to this bill as introduced and does
not reflect any amendments that may be subsequently adopted. If this bill
passes third reading in the house of introduction, a bill summary that
applies to the reengrossed version of this bill will be available at
http://leg.colorado.gov.)
The bill creates a diploma endorsement for bilingualism for
graduating high school students.
The bill changes educational requirements for the biliteracy
endorsement program. The bill allows students in school districts that do
not offer the biliteracy or the bilingualism program to access the program
through department-certified entities. The department of education is

2 SECTION 1. Legislative declaration. (1) The general assembly
3 finds and declares that:
4 (a) The purpose of updating Colorado's seal of biliteracy is to
5 modernize, clarify, and expand equitable access to Colorado's seal of
6 biliteracy for high school graduates while maintaining rigorous standards;
7 (b) Colorado's seal of biliteracy was established to recognize high
8 school graduates who demonstrate proficiency in English and at least one
9 additional world language. The seal signals readiness for college, careers,
10 and participation in a global economy.
11 (c) The implementation of the seal of biliteracy has varied widely
12 across school districts: Out of the 179 school districts in Colorado, only
13 55 of them offer the seal of biliteracy for high school diplomas. Many
14 students, particularly those in rural districts, small schools, or districts
15 without formal biliteracy programs, face barriers when accessing the seal
16 of biliteracy, despite demonstrating strong language skills.
17 (d) Biliteracy is defined as the proficiency in actively listening to,
18 speaking, understanding, reading, and writing in English and another
19 world language, while bilingualism is defined as the speaking, listening,
20 and understanding of another world language and English;
21 (e) In order to earn a seal of biliteracy, high school students
22 should be able to demonstrate speaking, listening, reading, and writing
23 proficiency in a world language in numerous ways, including in
24 coursework, assessments, or approved bodies of evidence;
25 (f) Colorado's public schools educate 114,482 multilingual
-2- 1028

1 learners. Expanding access to the seal of biliteracy and creating access to
2 a seal of bilingualism for high school graduates will demonstrate
3 Colorado's commitment to supporting all students.
4 (g) Multilingualism is a proven asset in business, health care,
5 education, government services, and national security. Local and global
6 industries in Colorado have unique demands for language skills, creating
7 distinct career opportunities for multilingual professionals. The seal of
8 biliteracy will allow high school graduates to prove their language skills
9 to potential employers and will ease their transition into the workforce
10 and higher education.

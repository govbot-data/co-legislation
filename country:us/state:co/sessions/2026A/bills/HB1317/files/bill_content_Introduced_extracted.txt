(transition plan). The transition committee shall begin meeting by July 1,
2026, and shall submit the transition plan to the joint budget committee
by November 1, 2026. The transition plan must include recommendations
about the structure of the department of higher education (department),

recommendations about transitioning various offices, agencies, programs,

department (executive director). The governor appoints, with the consent
of the senate, the executive director.

2 SECTION 1. Legislative declaration. (1) The general assembly
3 finds and declares that:
4 (a) Colorado has an economic and civic interest in ensuring that
5 Coloradans can access high-quality postsecondary opportunities;
6 (b) Higher education, workforce development, and employment
7 programs should empower every Coloradan to discover their strengths,
8 pursue their passions, meet the demands of a changing economy, and
9 build careers that provide economic security;
10 (c) Too many Coloradans get disconnected from workforce and
11 employment opportunity, and as a result, Colorado has struggled to train
12 and retain homegrown talent;
13 (d) Today, Coloradans must navigate a complex postsecondary
14 talent development system of 472 schools, more than 300 apprenticeship
15 programs, more than 4,500 eligible training programs, and nearly 10,000
16 degree options;
17 (e) More than 20 divisions, offices, and units across seven state
18 entities deliver 110 programs and initiatives in the system;
19 (f) This fragmentation makes opportunity difficult to find for

1 Coloradans, and it limits the reach and impact of state investments;
2 (g) Colorado must bring together, streamline, and create seamless
3 connections between education and skill-building pathways for
4 Coloradans;
5 (h) Colorado needs a purpose-built, unified department that can
6 easily connect all Coloradans, including underserved populations, to
7 opportunity through postsecondary education, skills training, and
8 employment; and
9 (i) We must boldly reimagine how our education, training, and
10 employment systems are serving Colorado learners, job seekers, workers,

CONCERNING CREATING A UNIFIED SYSTEM OF POSTSECONDARY TALENT
DEVELOPMENT, AND, IN CONNECTION THEREWITH, CREATING A
COMMITTEE TO DEVELOP A PLAN TO TRANSITION OVERSIGHT OF
WORKFORCE DEVELOPMENT PROGRAMS TO THE DEPARTMENT OF
HIGHER EDUCATION.
Be it enacted by the General Assembly oft he State ofC olorado:
SECTION 1. Legislative declaration. ( 1) The general assembly
finds and declares that:
(a) Colorado has an economic and civic interest in ensuring that
Coloradans can access high-quality postsecondary opportunities;
(b) Higher education, workforce development, adult education, and
employment programs should empower every Coloradan to discover their

strengths, pursue their passions, meet the demands of a changing economy,
and build careers that provide economic security;
(c) Too many Coloradans get disconnected from workforce and
employment opportunity, and as a result, Colorado has struggled to train
and retain homegrown talent;
( d) Adult learners, trainees, and job seekers may need to enter or
reenter the workforce system from diverse points in their lives based on
current needs and life status;
(e ) Today, Coloradans must navigate a complex postsecondary talent
development system of 472 schools, more than 300 apprenticeship
programs, more than 4,500 eligible training programs, 21 adult education
programs, and nearly 10,000 degree options;
(t) More than 20 divisions, offices, and units across seven state
entities deliver 110 programs and initiatives in the system;
(g) This fragmentation makes opportunity difficult to find for
Coloradans, and it limits the reach and impact of state investments;
(h) Colorado must bring together, streamline, and create seamless
connections between education and skill-building pathways for Coloradans;
(i) Colorado needs a purpose-built, unified department that can
easily connect all Coloradans, including underserved populations, to
opportunity through postsecondary education, skills training, and
employment;
G) Reimagining our workforce system should include consideration
of the connection between health and human services programs,
out-of-school time programs, and workforce programs; and
(k) We must boldly reimagine how our education, training, and
employment systems are serving Colorado learners, job seekers, workers,
and employers.

23-1.1-103. Executive director of the department-appointment
- duties - repeal.
(1) (a) THE GOVERNOR SHALL APPOINT, WITH THE CONSENT OF THE
SENATE, AN EXECUTIVE DIRECTOR QUALIFIED BY SUBSTANTIAL TRAINING
AND EXPERIENCE IN THE FIELD OF HIGHER EDUCATION AND WORKFORCE
DEVELOPMENT. THE EXECUTIVE DIRECTOR IS THE EXECUTIVE OFFICER OF
THE COMMISSION AND THE DEPARTMENT, SERVES AT THE PLEASURE OF THE
GOVERNOR, AND MUST RECEIVE COMPENSATION COMMENSURATE WITH THE
DUTIES OF THE OFFICE AS DETERMINED BY THE GOVERNOR. THE EXECUTIVE
DIRECTOR SHALL DISCHARGE THEIR DUTIES AND RESPONSIBILITIES IN
ACCORDANCE WITH THE POLICIES, PROCEDURES, AND DIRECTIVES OF THE
COMMISSION AND THE DEPARTMENT. THE EXECUTIVE DIRECTOR SHALL
EMPLOY PROFESSIONAL AND CLERICAL PERSONNEL AS THE EXECUTIVE
DIRECTOR DEEMS NECESSARY TO CARRY OUT THE DUTIES AND FUNCTIONS OF
THE COMMISSION AND THE DEPARTMENT. OFFICES HELD BY THE EXECUTIVE
DIRECTOR AND PROFESSIONAL PERSONNEL ARE DECLARED TO BE
EDUCATIONAL IN NATURE AND NOT UNDER THE STATE PERSONNEL SYSTEM.

GOVERNOR APPOINTS A DIFFERENT PERSON TO ASSUME THE OFFICE OF THE
EXECUTIVE DIRECTOR OF THE DEPARTMENT OF HIGHER EDUCATION
EFFECTIVE ON JULY 1, 2028.

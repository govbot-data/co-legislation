2 SECTION 1. Legislative declaration. (1) The general assembly
3 finds and declares that:
4 (a) Colorado has an economic and civic interest in ensuring that
5 Coloradans can access high-quality postsecondary opportunities;
6 (b) Higher education, workforce development, adult education,
7 and employment programs should empower every Coloradan to discover
8 their strengths, pursue their passions, meet the demands of a changing
9 economy, and build careers that provide economic security;
10 (c) Too many Coloradans get disconnected from workforce and
11 employment opportunity, and as a result, Colorado has struggled to train
12 and retain homegrown talent;
13 (d) Adult learners, trainees, and job seekers may need to enter or
14 reenter the workforce system from diverse points in their lives based on
15 current needs and life status;
16 (e) Today, Coloradans must navigate a complex postsecondary
17 talent development system of 472 schools, more than 300 apprenticeship
18 programs, more than 4,500 eligible training programs, 21 adult education
19 programs, and nearly 10,000 degree options;
-2- 1317

1 (f) More than 20 divisions, offices, and units across seven state
2 entities deliver 110 programs and initiatives in the system;
3 (g) This fragmentation makes opportunity difficult to find for
4 Coloradans, and it limits the reach and impact of state investments;
5 (h) Colorado must bring together, streamline, and create seamless
6 connections between education and skill-building pathways for
7 Coloradans;
8 (i) Colorado needs a purpose-built, unified department that can
9 easily connect all Coloradans, including underserved populations, to
10 opportunity through postsecondary education, skills training, and

12 (j) Reimagining our workforce system should include
13 consideration of the connection between health and human services

CONCERNING DESIGNATION OF A PORTION OF U.S.
HIGHWAY 34 BETWEEN WILSON AVE. AND NORTH COUNTY
RD. 23H IN MEMORY OF SGT. JOHN "JACK" THURMAN.
WHEREAS, Sergeant John "Jack" Ryland Thurman, United States
Marine Corps, was born on September 27, 1925, in South Dakota, and
passed February 1, 2025, at 99 years old, in Loveland; and
WHEREAS, Jack married the love of his life, Carol Jean Meier, on
October 22, 1951, and together, the couple raised four children; and
WHEREAS, After many decades together, Carol Jean passed June 22,
2009; and
WHEREAS, Sgt. Thurman was a veteran of World War II, a survivor
of Iwo Jima, and one of just a few select soldiers pictured in the iconic
"Gung Ho" photo of U.S. Marines atop Japan's Mt. Suribachi mid-battle;
and

WHEREAS, Jack enlisted on his 18th birthday with the United States
Marine Corps at Minnesota's Ft. Snelling; was selected for special forces
training at Marine Corps Base Camp Pendleton, CA, a group that became
known as the original "Carlson's Raiders", and was attached to the 5th
Marine Division 27th Regiment, 2nd Battalion, Foxtrot Company, and
Headquarters; and
WHEREAS, Sgt. Thurman deployed to the Japanese island of Iwo
Jima from February 19, 1945, to the end of March 1945, where he was in
the first waves of steel "amtracs" (amphibious assault vehicles) that
landed on Red Beach 1; and
WHEREAS, An advanced infantry sniper, Sgt. Thurman provided
coverage for the six original Iwo Jima flag raisers, saved numerous
Marines by securing pillboxes and Japanese tunnels, located a strategic
map identifying Japanese heavy artillery locations-which impacted the
outcome of the war, provided supplies to the front lines, and rescued
sailors and Marines from a downed B-29 bomber on Motoyama Airfield
No. 1 while under direct fire; and
WHEREAS, Sgt. Thurman took mortar wounds to his hip and face
during the battle, and his arms numbed from the impact of his M1
Carbine rifle itself taking a hit, but he was determined to keep moving
forward; and
WHEREAS, For his heroism, Sgt. Thurman received a Bronze Star
Medal, Good Conduct Medal, American Campaign Medal,
Asiatic-Pacific Campaign Medal, and the World War II Victory Medal,
in addition to an Honorable Discharge; and
WHEREAS, Sgt. Thurman's military memorabilia has been preserved
at museums such as the National Museum of the Marine Corps near
Quantico, VA; the World War II Memorial in Washington, D.C.; the
Center for American Values in Pueblo; and the Broomfield Veterans
Museum; and
WHEREAS, After World War II, Sgt. Thurman had a successful
career as an architect and designer in Colorado, best known for his
involvement with the University of Colorado Engineering Center, the

U.S. Marine Corps Memorial in Golden, the Admiral Arleigh Burke
Memorial in Boulder, the Black Forest restaurant in Nederland, and
several commercial buildings throughout the United States and in
Germany; and
WHEREAS, Sgt. Thurman remained dedicated to community service
as a member or volunteer with organizations including the Boulder and
Loveland VFW and American Legion-for which he was appointed
Commander and Colorado District Commander, Arvada-based Marine
Corps club "Cooper's Troopers", the Marine Corps League, 40/8, Lions
Club, Knights of Columbus, Veterans Honor Guard, youth sports, Boy
Scouts and Girl Scouts, police and fire departments, and as a guest
speaker providing numerous presentations educating children and adults
alike; and
WHEREAS, Sgt. Thurman served as Grand Marshal of the Larimer
County Fair Parade on July 27, 2019, a day subsequently proclaimed as
"Sgt. John 'Jack' Thurman Day" by the Larimer County Board of
Commissioners; was Veterans Day Grand Marshal for Denver, Loveland,
Boulder, and Longmont; and was selected as "Veteran of the Month"
twice by FOX31; and
WHEREAS, Sgt. Thurman was also honored by President Donald
Trump in 2020 during a Colorado Springs rally, recognizing him for his
service as an Iwo Jima survivor; and
WHEREAS, In addition to his military and civic accomplishments,
Sgt. Thurman also was a champion chess player at the University of
Colorado and an established author, completed the Bolder Boulder in
2016 in a wheelchair pushed by his daughter Karen, and tossed the coin
for both a Colorado Buffaloes and a Denver Broncos game; and
WHEREAS, Sgt. Thurman also served as a lifelong inspiration for his
daughter, Commander Karen Thurman, U.S. Navy (Ret.), who herself
served 21 years on active duty, with deployments to Bosnia; Naval
Station Guantanamo Bay, Cuba; Okinawa; and Iraq; and
WHEREAS, Sgt. Thurman, a member of the Greatest Generation, will
be remembered for his heroism, courage, dedication, strong faith, humble
compassion, and his significant impact on our American history; now,

therefore,
Be It Resolved by the Senate of the Seventy-fifth General Assembly of
the State of Colorado, the House of Representatives concurring herein:
(1) That the portion of U.S. Highway 34 between Wilson Ave. and
North County Rd. 23H be designated as the "Sgt. John 'Jack' Thurman
Memorial Highway";
(2) That the Colorado Department of Transportation may accept and
expend gifts, grants, and donations for the purpose of the initial
placement of signs to mark the portion of U.S. Highway 34 between
Wilson Ave. and North County Rd. 23H as the "Sgt. John 'Jack' Thurman
Memorial Highway"; and
(3) That the Colorado Department of Transportation may explore a
cooperative agreement with the City of Loveland, Larimer County, or
both, for the maintenance of the signs marking the "Sgt. John 'Jack'
Thurman Memorial Highway".
Be It Further Resolved, That copies of this Joint Resolution be sent to
Commander Karen Thurman, Larimer County Commissioner John
Kefalas, and Colorado Department of Transportation Executive Director
Shoshana Lew.

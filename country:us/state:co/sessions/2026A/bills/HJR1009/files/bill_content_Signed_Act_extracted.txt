CONCERNING VETERANS MENTAL HEALT HAWA RENESS.
WHEREAS, Colorado is home to almost 350,000 veterans and 50,000
uniformed military personnel, and Colorado has one oft he highest suicide
rates among our veterans in the nation; and
WHEREAS, In 2022, 178 veterans committed suicide in Colorado,
which is significantly higher than the national suicide rate among
civilians; and
WHEREAS, Veterans honorably serve their country with courage and
oftentimes return home with mental health barriers that hinder their
transition to the civilian workforce; and
WHEREAS, The Colorado General Assembly has increased access to
mental health and addiction treatment and expanded wraparound social
services to include employment, education, and housing, which have
made positive impacts on numerous veterans' lives and helped make the
transition to civilian life more seamless; and

WHEREAS, Innovative community response services have
demonstrated measurable impacts in saving veterans' lives over the past
three years; and
WHEREAS, Organizations like Next Chapter. and Mt. Carmel
Veterans Service Center in Colorado Springs have enrolled more than
1,200 veterans in mental health services during the past three years, and
no lives have been lost to suicide among the enrollees; now, therefore,
Be It Resolved by the House of Representatives of the Seventy-fifth
General Assembly oft he State ofC olorado, the Senate concurring herein:
( 1) That we commit to continuing to support our veterans by
recognizing the obstacles they face when they return to civilian life. We
recommit ourselves to supporting our veterans by expanding services and
breaking down stigmas.
(2) That we recognize the significant enduring sacrifice our veterans,
Gold Star families, and uniformed personnel have made for our freedom
and security, and we commit to expanding the community support. that
demonstrates our persistent gratitude for their service to our way of life;
and

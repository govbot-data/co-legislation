CONCERNING THE CELEBRATION OF LUNAR NEW YEAR
2026.
WHEREAS, Colorado became the second U.S. state to recognize
Lunar New Year Day, which became an observed state holiday in 2024
after the passage ofH ouse Bill 23-1271, which designated the first Friday
of every February as an observed state holiday; and
WHEREAS, The official observation of Lunar New Year Day in
Colorado was made possible by the work of Vietnamese refugee and
founder and chair of the Lunar New Year Allies Advisory Group, Nga
Vuong-Sandoval, who spearheaded the efforts to establish the state
holiday; and
WHEREAS, Lunar New Year allows Coloradans the opportunity to
celebrate Asian American culture and recognize an important part of the
fabric of United States diversity; and
WHEREAS, Lunar New Year can be traced back to the fourteenth

century B. C. in China and is based on the lunar calendar; and
WHEREAS, The beginning of the Lunar New Year usually
commences between late January and mid-February and is one of the
most important holidays for many Asian American communities; and
WHEREAS, Lunar New Year 2026, the Year of the Horse, will be
celebrated on February 17; and
WHEREAS, Asians and the Asian diaspora have expanded the Lunar
New Year celebration into an annual, week-long, worldwide event that is
celebrated by about two-thirds of Asian Americans and many Asians and
non-Asians from China, Vietnam, Taiwan,· South Korea, Malaysia,
Indonesia, the Philippines, and many other places; and
WHEREAS, Over two billion people celebrate Lunar New Year
worldwide, with many returning home to spend time with family and
share blessings of being together with loved ones; and
WHEREAS, During Lunar New Year, it is customary to wish good
fortune, health, prosperity, and happiness on those around you; the
holiday is celebrated in the United States and across the globe; and
WHEREAS, Asian Americans have served in the Colorado General
Assembly in the state's 150 years, including Filipino American Rep.
Dennis Apuan (2009-2011) and currently serving Vietnamese American
Rep. Kenny Van Nguy en, and Asian Americans currently serve
throughout state government, including Japanese American Acting
Agriculture Commissioner Robert Sakata and Office of New Americans
Director Dee Daniels Scriven; and
WHEREAS, The Asian American community is the fastest growing
population in the United States, and the popularity of the holiday
continues to grow; and
WHEREAS, The Colorado General Assembly's exemplary enactment
of an observed Lunar New Year holiday and continual recognition of
Asian culture, holiday, cuisine, and traditions, as well as working across
party lines, has had a very real and positive impact on Asian American
Coloradans and brought peace, prosperity, and goodwill throughout the

state; now, therefore,
Be It Resolved by the Senate oft he Seventy-fifth General Assembly of
the State of Colorado, the House ofR epresentatives concurring herein:
(1) That we, the members oft he Colorado GeneralAssembly,join our
Asian communities in celebrating Lunar New Year;
(2) That we invite Coloradans to attend events that celebrate Lunar
New Year and promote local Asian American communities;
(3) That we honor and respect our Asian American community
members, particularly for their entrepreneurialism, commitment to
innovating and advancing new technologies, strong family values, and
culture;
(4 ) That increasing the visibility of cultural traditions that are widely
celebrated among the Asian diaspora would serve as a counterbalance to
the marginalization that many in the Asian American community have
encountered over the past several years; and
( 5) That it is appropriate to recognize the cultural and historical
significance of Lunar New Year and acknowledge all individuals who
celebrate this significant occasion by reaffirming Colorado's commitment
to recognizing and celebrating Asian Americans and their contributions
to our great state.

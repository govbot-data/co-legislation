2 WHEREAS, According to the 2024 Colorado College State of the
3 Rockies poll, 88 percent of Westerners visited national public lands in the
4 previous year, with nearly one-fourth visiting more than 10 times, and 84
5 percent of Coloradans support the creation of new national parks, national
6 monuments, and national wildlife refuges; and
7 WHEREAS, Our public lands provide important wildlife habitats,
8 clean water, clean air, and natural amenities that contribute to Coloradans'
9 quality of life and Colorado's climate resiliency; and
10 WHEREAS, In 2016, through Senate Bill 16-021, Colorado
11 created a state holiday to recognize and pay tribute to our public lands:
12 Public Lands Day on the third Saturday of each May; and
13 WHEREAS, The effective stewardship of these lands requires a
14 robust partnership between the federal government and the American
15 public, ensuring decisions reflect the diverse interests and values of all
16 citizens, regardless of location or background; and
17 WHEREAS, Bedrock federal laws such as the "Federal Land
18 Policy and Management Act of 1976" (FLPMA) and the "National
19 Environmental Policy Act of 1969" (NEPA) were enacted to define the
20 federal government's fiduciary duty to the American people, requiring
21 balanced resource management, ensuring that federal decisions are
22 informed by public input, and protecting the long-term, sustainable use
23 of public lands; and
24 WHEREAS, Recent actions at the federal level, including
25 significant policy changes, the reduction of on-the-ground management
26 resources, and the streamlining of regulatory processes, risk undermining
27 the bedrock principles of effective public land stewardship; and
28 WHEREAS, While not an overt sell-off, these cumulative actions,
29 such as diminishing public engagement opportunities, shortening review
30 timelines, and increasing regulatory instability, have the troubling effect
31 of centralizing power in Washington, D.C., while marginalizing the
32 voices of local communities and land users; and
33 WHEREAS, This shift toward a less accountable management
34 structure reduces transparency and legal durability, disproportionately

1 favoring certain expedited private interests over the long-term, balanced
2 needs of the public, thereby creating a de facto loss of the public's access,
3 involvement, and recourse regarding the future of their shared national
4 assets; and
5 WHEREAS, Protecting the integrity of the public process and
6 ensuring fair access to our shared national resources are fundamentally
7 nonpartisan issues that uphold the democratic ideals embodied by our
8 public land system; now, therefore,
9 Be It Resolved by the Senate of the Seventy-fifth General Assembly
10 of the State of Colorado, the House of Representatives concurring herein:
11 That we, the members of the Colorado General Assembly:
12 (1) Declare our unwavering support for maintaining our national
13 public lands under the stewardship of the United States government and
14 the federal land management agencies, and for the foundational public
15 processes designed to guide their sustainable use;
16 (2) Oppose all efforts, whether legislative or administrative, that
17 directly or indirectly diminish the public's voice, access, and recourse in
18 the management of national public lands, including the erosion of
19 bedrock laws such as the "National Environmental Policy Act of 1969"
20 (NEPA) and the "Federal Land Policy and Management Act of 1976"
21 (FLPMA); and
22 (3) Call upon the members of Colorado's congressional
23 delegation, the governor, and the attorney general to take all necessary
24 actions to defend the public process and statutory safeguards that ensure
25 public lands are managed for the broadest possible public benefit, rather
26 than being effectively privatized or subjugated to narrow special interests.

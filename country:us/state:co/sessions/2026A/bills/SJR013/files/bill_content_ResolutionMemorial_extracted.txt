CONCERNING THE RECOGNITION OF YOUTH MENTAL
HEALTH ACTION DAY.
WHEREAS, The youth of Colorado are calling upon Colorado's
elected officials to prioritize policies that support youth mental health and
well-being; and
WHEREAS, Colorado is facing a youth mental health crisis, which
has long-term ripple effects on our state's education, public safety,
carceral, and economic systems; and
WHEREAS, Colorado ranks 41st in the nation for youth mental health
per the nonprofit Mental Health America; and
WHEREAS, Pediatric professionals who support youth found in the
2023 Healthy Kids Colorado Survey that about 1 in 4 Colorado high
school students reported feeling consistently sad or hopeless; and

WHEREAS, Despite the department of public health and
environment's office of suicide prevention finding decreases in Colorado
youth seriously considering suicide and planning suicide attempts in
recent years, our state still sees disproportionately higher rates of suicide
compared to the rest of the country, with suicide being a leading cause of
death among Colorado children under 18; and
WHEREAS, Data from the National Library of Medicine shows that
between 10% and 16% of young children (ages 2 to 8) have a diagnosed
mental, behavioral, or developmental disorder; and
WHEREAS, More than 1 in 5 middle school students reported poor
mental health in the 2023 Healthy Kids Colorado Survey; and
WHEREAS, Children and youth face stigmas related to mental health
needs that may prevent them from seeking the support and services they
need; and
WHEREAS, Research from the Colorado School of Public Health
found that early identification and treatment of mental health needs
improve outcomes for children and families and reduces long-term
societal costs; and
WHEREAS, The State Health Professional Shortage Areas map shows
that continued focus is critical to fully integrate primary and mental health
care, as many communities still lack sufficient services to ensure that
children and youth receive the right care at the right time; and
WHEREAS, Sustained initiatives are needed to expand the continuum
of care from early preventive support to high-acuity care for Colorado
children and youth with complex mental health needs; and
WHEREAS, Pediatric health-care providers play a vital role in
identifying, supporting, and treating the mental health needs of children

and youth across Colorado; now, therefore,
Be It Resolved by the Senate of the Seventy-fifth General Assembly of
the State of Colorado, the House of Representatives concurring herein:
(1) That the General Assembly commits to promoting child and youth
well-being in Colorado through cost-effective investments that maintain
a functional mental health system capable of addressing the unique needs
of children;
(2) That the General Assembly supports continued efforts to
strengthen coordination, efficiency, and accessibility within the children's
mental health system;
(3) That the General Assembly continues to partner with mental
health providers and professionals, state agencies, local partners,
community organizations, and Colorado children, youth, and families to
prioritize the mental health needs in our state; and
(4) The General Assembly recognizes February 26, 2026, as Youth
Mental Health Action Day, where we honor the tireless efforts of

Colorado kids' mental health and well-being.

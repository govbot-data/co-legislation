CONCERNING SEXUAL ASSAULT AWARENESS MONTH,
AND, IN CONNECTION THEREWITH, RECOGNIZING APRIL AS
SEXUAL ASSAULT AWARENESS MONTH AND DESIGNATING
APRIL 29, 2026, AS COLORADO DENIM DAY.
WHEREAS, Sexual Assault Awareness Month (SAAM) officially
began in 2001, though the movement to raise awareness for and put an
end to sexual violence can be traced back much further; and
WHEREAS, Decades of activism and persistence by survivors and
advocates led to the founding of the first rape crisis center in the United
States in 1971 and the passage of legislation and funding to support
survivors, including the "Violence Against Women Act of 1994"
(VAWA); and

WHEREAS, The Denim Day campaign was sparked by the 1998
Italian Supreme Court ruling that overturned a rape conviction based on
the flawed argument that the victim's tight jeans indicated that she had
helped her rapist remove them, thereby implying her consent; and
WHEREAS, The Italian Supreme Court's ruling perpetuated a
harmful, victim-blaming mentality, and in response, women in the Italian
Parliament wore jeans to work the following day in solidarity with the
victim; and
WHEREAS, The following year, the Denim Day campaign launched
in the United States, encouraging individuals to wear jeans as a visible
symbol of protest against attitudes condoning sexual assault; and
WHEREAS, The Colorado Coalition Against Sexual Assault
(CCASA) has served as the collective voice for sexual assault survivors
and service providers in public policy initiatives, education, training, and
collaborative efforts in Colorado for over four decades and has worked
with the Colorado General Assembly to pass laws that provide protection
and support for survivors of sexual assault; and
WHEREAS, Since 2013, CCASA has hosted Colorado Denim Day,
continuing the movement for increased visibility and improved social and
political representation for sexual assault survivors in Colorado; and
WHEREAS, Sexual assault is a widespread and pervasive issue across
the United States, which is compounded by structural and systemic
barriers that put marginalized communities at even greater risk and less
able to access justice and healing; and
WHEREAS, Survivors who come forward show extraordinary
courage and resilience by facing challenges that include stigma, fear of
retaliation, and systemic barriers to justice; and
WHEREAS, Sexual Assault Awareness Month provides an
opportunity to both recognize the strength of survivors and highlight the
failures of the justice and legal system to take sexual assault and
exploitation seriously, including the urgent need for greater transparency,
accountability, and meaningful action in response to all forms of sexual
violence; and

WHEREAS, Support services are a critical mechanism to stabilize and
assist survivors, providing healing, harm prevention services, and
lifesaving interventions; and
WHEREAS, System- and community-based victim support service
providers across Colorado are currently facing an urgent funding crisis
resulting in detrimental cuts to programming while survivor need is
growing and increasing in complexity, leaving critical service gaps and
resulting in further harm; and
WHEREAS, Continued action is needed to protect and expand these
support services, treat survivors with respect and care, increase
accountability, and prevent sexual violence so that we can improve the
safety of our communities and continue doing what we do in denim
without fear of experiencing sexual violence; and
WHEREAS, Recognizing Sexual Assault Awareness Month and
Denim Day and bringing attention to the ongoing efforts and urgent need
to put an end to sexual violence remain critically important; now,
therefore,
Be It Resolved by the House of Representatives of the Seventy-fifth
General Assembly oft he State ofC olorado, the Senate concurring herein:

That we, the members of the Colorado General Assembly, recognize
April as Sexual Assault Awareness Month and designate April 29, 2026,
as Colorado Denim Day, a statewide event aligning with Denim Day
events across the world, and we are proud to recognize CCASA and other
organizations that work to unite Colorado communities in support of
ending sexual violence.

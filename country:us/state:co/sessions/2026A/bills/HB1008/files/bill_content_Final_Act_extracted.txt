CONCERNING MEASURES TO ENHANCE OUTDOOR RECREATION
OPPORTUNITIES IN THE STATE, AND, IN CONNECTION THEREWITH,
EXPANDING THE DIVISION OF PARKS AND WILDLIFE'S CAPACITY FOR
OUTDOOR RECREATION COORDINATION, PLANNING, AND
MANAGEMENT, AND MAKING AN APPROPRIATION.
Be it enacted by the General Assembly of the State of Colorado:
SECTION 1. Short title. The short title of this act is the "Colorado
Outdoor Opportunities Act".
SECTION 2. Legislative declaration. (1) The general assembly
finds and declares that:
________

through words or numbers indicate deletions from existing law and such material is not part of
the act.

(a) It is the policy of the state of Colorado that the state's
unparalleled landscapes, wildlife, public lands, and outdoor experiences be
protected, conserved, and enhanced for the benefit and enjoyment of current
and future generations, while protecting the state's agricultural heritage and
private property rights;
(b) Outdoor recreation, access to quality outdoor experiences, and
the conservation of lands and wildlife habitats, all within the context of a
changing climate and growing population, are vital components in ensuring
a thriving future for Colorado's outdoors;
(c) Outdoor recreation is fundamental to Colorado's economy,
culture, and identity, contributing to public health, community well-being,
and the development of lifelong connections to the outdoors. Outdoor
recreation annually contributes $65.8 billion to Colorado's economy and
supports 404,000 jobs, over 12% of the entire labor force in Colorado.
(d) Outdoor recreation's significant economic, fiscal, and community
benefits across the state include revenue that supports public services,
infrastructure, and investments, from transportation to child care and mental
health services, that contribute to the health, safety, and welfare of
Coloradans. It is important that these benefits be equitably distributed
throughout the state to the extent possible.
(e) Ensuring safe, high-quality outdoor experiences through
purposeful planning, coordination, and management is essential to
sustaining the state's outdoor heritage;
(f) Connecting youth to outdoor experiences is vital for ensuring the
long-term well-being of the state's residents, since research demonstrates
that outdoor engagement supports improved cognitive abilities, fosters
creativity and resilience, and is essential for promoting public health and
reducing stress, and many communities face barriers to accessing the
outdoors and the benefits of spending time in nature;
(g) Colorado's outdoors face significant and urgent pressures from
growing populations, human disturbance, and development, alongside
severe climate-driven impacts such as wildfires, drought, and rising
temperatures. It is essential that the state play an active role in appropriately
managing these pressures to ensure Colorado's outdoors are available for
PAGE 2-HOUSE BILL 26-1008

future generations.
(h) Thoughtful planning and management of outdoor recreation and
associated infrastructure should consider economic, fiscal, and
service-related impacts on local communities and regions and can support
conservation goals by reducing resource impacts, improving stewardship,
and sustaining the long-term health of the state's public lands;
(i) Colorado has a long-standing tradition of managing public lands
and working landscapes to support a range of compatible uses and affirms
that coordinated outdoor recreation management can occur alongside
conservation, agricultural operations, and other land uses in ways that
support long-term resource stewardship and community values;
(j) As more visitors and Coloradans participate in all forms of
outdoor recreation, it is important to advance exceptional recreation
experiences that are accessible, inclusive, and sustainable so that future
generations may continue to enjoy the state's world-class outdoor
opportunities;
(k) To effectively manage these challenges, the state must formalize
and strengthen collaboration across conservation, wildlife, agriculture, and
recreation partners, local governments, Tribal governments, and federal
land managers, including by soliciting rural perspectives; develop shared
tools and data; advance coordinated planning that recognizes that
Colorado's public and private lands support multiple uses; and align funding
to support voluntary, strategic actions that sustain high-quality recreation
opportunities while maintaining resilient landscapes, wildlife habitat,
working lands, and community values for all Coloradans;
(l) Colorado's outdoors strategy, developed by the department of
natural resources, the division of parks and wildlife, great outdoors
Colorado, the office of climate preparedness in the governor's office, and
the outdoor recreation industry office in the office of economic
development, in collaboration with partners and stakeholders, provides the
necessary statewide vision and coordination to advance conservation,
climate resilience, and outdoor recreation opportunities. One strength of
Colorado's outdoors strategy is its voluntary approach that allows partners
to leverage opportunities that support locally determined needs.

(m) The general assembly recognizes the role that the Colorado
outdoor regional partnerships initiative, created by the governor's executive
order B 2020 008, plays in convening vital perspectives, partners, and
expertise in land conservation, land use, and recreation planning. The
initiative is a locally driven and powerful platform for early and proactive
coordination among stakeholders, helping to identify shared priorities,
reduce conflict, and advance tangible outcomes for outdoor recreation and
conservation statewide. It is the intent of the general assembly that the
initiative remains a voluntary and locally derived process with opportunities
for stakeholder engagement, including county governments.
(n) The division of parks and wildlife engages and will continue to
engage in formal consultation with Tribal governments, including the
Southern Ute Indian Tribe and Ute Mountain Ute Tribe, which are the two
federally recognized tribes with reservations in Colorado, when the division
is requested to do so and when issues of importance to Tribal governments
arise, which issues concern outdoor recreation or division actions on state
public lands that may affect Tribal sovereignty or treaty rights of the Tribal
governments in Colorado, including history, culture, and natural and
cultural resources;
(o) Meaningful engagement with tribal nations is important, and the
collaborative framework developed in Colorado's outdoors strategy is
crucial to support ongoing tribal participation in statewide outdoor
recreation and conservation efforts;
(p) The outdoor recreation industry office plays a vital role in
advancing outdoor recreation. The mission of the office is to champion
industry, communities, and people through Colorado's great outdoors. The
office is a vital partner for the division of parks and wildlife in coordinating
outdoor recreation industry partnerships and supporting the division in
planning and outcomes for the advancement of exceptional and sustainable
outdoor recreation opportunities.
(q) Local governments are critical partners in outdoor recreation
planning, coordination, and management, and recreation impacts
communities in different ways. The rapid increase in demand for outdoor
recreation is challenging local governments' ability to maintain access and
provide related services. For example, counties and municipalities
frequently experience increased and unique demands related to outdoor
PAGE 4-HOUSE BILL 26-1008

recreation, including emergency services. Counties are well suited to
convene area partners and play a critical role in identifying and addressing
localized impacts of recreation, and state coordination should incorporate
local perspectives to reduce unintended burdens on community
infrastructure and services. It is essential that the state work in partnership
with local governments to manage these impacts and advance locally driven
visions for outdoor recreation.
(r) With approximately 60% of Colorado land in private ownership,
working with private landowners is also essential to the success of
Colorado's outdoors strategy, and it is important to ensure that agricultural
interests, private landowners, including private landowners adjacent to
outdoor recreation development, and local governments are integrally
involved in state efforts; wildlife habitats are protected; and private property
rights are upheld as conservation and recreation management decisions are
made;
(s) The division of parks and wildlife possesses a long history of
outdoor recreation planning, management, and recreational asset delivery
through its state parks system, which serves as a significant source of
exceptional and accessible outdoor recreation opportunities. The division's
responsibilities extend beyond state park boundaries and encompass outdoor
recreation coordination, wildlife conservation, and support for partners
managing lands across all jurisdictions. As a leader in outdoor recreation
management, the division is well-positioned to play a critical coordinating
role in advancing outdoor recreation planning and management statewide,
beyond the boundaries of its current 43 state parks. It is the intent of the
general assembly to formalize the division's role in meeting these
responsibilities, without diminishing the division's long-standing
responsibilities.
(t) The division of parks and wildlife assumes lead responsibility as
the state's coordinating entity for advancing implementation of Colorado's
outdoors strategy, in partnership with the department of natural resources,
great outdoors Colorado, the outdoor recreation industry office, the office
of climate preparedness, and other state and local government partners. In
this role, the division facilitates early and proactive coordination and
planning among agencies and stakeholders to reduce conflict, improve
efficiency, and ensure accountability in advancing its goals and vision to
find mutually beneficial outcomes that support and maintain Colorado's
PAGE 5-HOUSE BILL 26-1008

world-class outdoor recreation and natural resources.
(2) The general assembly further finds the need to expand and
strengthen the division of parks and wildlife's capacity for the coordination,
planning, development, and management of Colorado's outdoor recreation
to ensure the state's recreation opportunities provide high-quality visitor
experiences that are responsibly managed to align natural resources,
wildlife, agricultural heritage, private property, and public safety. The
general assembly further recognizes that 36% of Colorado is federal public
land and a significant amount of recreation in Colorado occurs on federal
public lands; therefore, there is a growing need for the division to support
recreation management and stewardship on federal public lands, which
necessitates increased staff capacity and enhanced coordination with federal
land management agencies, without expenditures from the wildlife cash
fund created in section 33-1-112 (1)(a), Colorado Revised Statutes.

CONCERNING THE RECOGNITION OF ENVIRONMENTAL
INITIATIVES.
WHEREAS, Coloradans have long loved the great outdoors, and we
take tremendous pride in celebrating our natural heritage and being
stewards of the environment for future generations; and
WHEREAS, Coloradans have played leading roles in advocating for
environmental conservation and raising awareness for being good
stewards through local, state, national, and international awareness
initiatives; and
WHEREAS, John McConnell, a peace activist who resided in Denver
until his death in 2012, established the concept of Earth Day in March
1970;and
WHEREAS, Soon afterward, in April 1970, U.S. Senator Gaylord
Nelson organized an environmental teach-in, which has since evolved
into the popular Earth Day celebrated on April 22 of each year in the
United States and throughout much of the world; and

WHEREAS, April 22, 2026, is the 56th annual celebration of Earth
Day, an occasion that draws attention to environmental issues and
programs, from recycling, energy efficiency, and conservation efforts in
Colorado communities to global environmental initiatives; and
WHEREAS, More than one billion people in over 190 countries
participate in Earth Day annually as a recommitment to the goals of a
healthy environment and a peaceful, just, and sustainable world; and
WHEREAS, On December 14, 2022, the United Nations General
Assembly adopted a binding resolution put forth by 106 countries
proclaiming International Day of Zero Waste be held on March 30 of
every year; and
WHEREAS, International Day of Zero Waste is an initiative to raise
awareness ofw aste-reduction efforts, promote sustainable production and
consumption, and encourage cities and rural areas alike to adopt
sustainable and environmentally sound management of waste, including
local and national waste-reduction initiatives; and
WHEREAS, This year's International Day of Zero Waste is focused
on food waste, with governments asked to explore how they can help
limit the nearly one billion tons of edible food thrown away each year
through adopting policies related to food circularity, composting, and
food measurement and monitoring; and
WHEREAS, The state of Colorado and our citizens recognize the
importance of clean air, outdoor recreation, and wildlife to the state's
economy, identity, and public health and are therefore committed to the
conservation and stewardship ofo ur natural landscape, open space, rivers,
mountains, lakes, and sensitive ecosystems in accordance with celebrating
Earth Day; and
WHEREAS, Colorado fosters and encourages the development of
renewable energy to increase Colorado's supply of power and to bring
jobs and economic development to communities across the state; and
WHEREAS, Colorado has become a national leader in sustainable
living by instituting standards to ensure a prepared and educated
workforce to compete in the new energy economy; and

WHEREAS, Colorado has built a framework of public taxpayer
support for the new energy economy through the use of financial
incentives, including sales tax exemptions and refunds for renewable
energy investments, property tax exemptions for renewable energy
production, tax incentives to encourage the purchase of more
energy-efficient vehicles, and low-interest loans to help schools invest in
energy efficiency and renewable energy production; and
WHEREAS, The Colorado House ofR epresentatives supports efforts
to deploy technologies that offer operational savings, customer-choice
energy management, improved grid reliability, greater energy efficiency
and conservation options, and increased use ofrenewable energy sources;
and
WHEREAS, The House of Representatives supports policies that
create vibrant neighborhoods, promote public transportation, and rely on
the principles of smart growth and community sustainability; and
WHEREAS, The United States is the world's largest generator of
municipal solid waste, producing 12% of all municipal solid waste
while accounting for 4% of the population; and
WHEREAS, Data from the Global Waste Index showed Colorado
averaged 1.046 kilograms of municipal solid waste generated per person,
the 30th-highest state ranking in the United States; and
WHEREAS, Colorado's rate of municipal waste diversion was 15.7%
in 2024, the most recent year for which data is available from the state,
trailing far behind the adopted statewide goals of 35% by 2026 and 45%
by 2036; and
WHEREAS, Recent research from Eco-Cycle found that Colorado's
rate of composting waste was less than half of the national average of
32% and among the lowest-ranked states in the nation; and
WHEREAS, The Colorado department of public health and
environment reports that approximately 30% to 40% of all food produced
goes uneaten; and

WHEREAS, Producing food requires significant water, energy, and
land use, resources that are wasted when food is thrown away; and
WHEREAS, Approximately 60% of uneaten food is sent to landfills,
where it decomposes and produces methane; and
WHEREAS, The Colorado General Assembly has already signaled its
support for food-waste initiatives through the passage of House Bill
25-1166, which required the department ofp ublic health and environment
to provide annual training on food waste prevention and reduction
strategies and develop a food waste reduction guidance document; and
WHEREAS, We acknowledge that, to limit waste and protect
Colorado's natural resources and landscapes, our state, and our Earth, we,
as elected representatives of the state, must provide leadership, tap into
the expertise and talent of our community, and engage the hearts and
minds of all citizens; now, therefore,
Be It Resolved by the House of Representatives of the Seventy-fifth
General Assembly oft he State of Colorado:
That we, the members of the Colorado House of Representatives,
reaffirm our commitment to the goals and principles of our
responsibilities as stewards oft he Earth. In recognition of2026 Earth Day
and International Day of Zero Waste, and in keeping with our moral
obligation to limit waste and protect and enhance the long-term health of
the state's natural environment, economy, and community, we support
Earth Day and International Day of Zero Waste by:
( 1) Inviting and encouraging all citizens, businesses, organizations,
schools, clubs, congregations, neighborhoods, and families to participate
in community International Day ofZ ero Waste events on March 30, 2026,
and Earth Day activities on April 22, 2026, and to engage in
environmentally sound and waste-reducing practices in their everyday
lives;
(2) Using the observance of International Day of Zero Waste and
Earth Day as a springboard for exploring new avenues for sustainability
and waste reduction;

(3) Demonstrating leadership and continually evaluating state
operations, facilities, and services to ensure the highest standard of
efficiency, sustainability, waste reduction, and environmental protection;
(4 ) Forging partnerships with local organizations, environmental
professionals, businesses, and citizens to encourage the adoption of
statewide sustainability practices and the development of green
technologies, and become a model for the region and country; and
( 5) Encouraging teachers and parents to emphasize environmental
awareness among young people so that they have the knowledge to make
sound choices in their daily lives to preserve and enhance our natural
environment and are able to join the workforce of our state with a strong
environmental ethic.
Be It Further Resolved, That copies of this Resolution be sent to
Colorado's Congressional Delegation, Governor Jared Polis, Lieutenant
Governor Dianne Primavera, the City ofBoulder, the County of Boulder,
the University of Colorado Boulder, and Eco-Cycle.

3 I. During World War II, Latina/o contributions in the Armed Forces

5 WHEREAS, Nearly 500,000 Latina/o Americans, many of them
6 women and the children of immigrants, fought for the cause of freedom
7 in World War II, among them the 158th Regimental Combat Team,
8 nicknamed the "Bushmasters", and Company E of the 141st Infantry
9 Regiment, which consisted entirely of Mexican-American soldiers; and
10 WHEREAS, In 1948, in the aftermath of World War II, Dr. Hector
11 Garcia founded the American GI Forum to ensure educational, medical,
12 housing, and other benefits and services for Latina/o veterans. Dr. Garcia
13 and the American GI Forum worked diligently to secure equal access to
14 benefits, and in 1984 Dr. Garcia was the first Mexican American to
15 receive the Presidential Medal of Freedom; and
16 WHEREAS, In the Korean War, the Puerto Rican 65th Infantry
17 Regiment, also known as "the Borinqueneers", served with distinction and
18 valor; for their efforts, they were awarded two Presidential Unit Citations,
19 a Meritorious Unit Commendation, and two Republic of Korea
20 Presidential Unit Citations. In 2014, the unit was recognized with a
21 Congressional Gold Medal; and
22 WHEREAS, During the Vietnam War, more than 80,000 Latina/o
23 service members served, 22 of whom were awarded the Congressional
24 Medal of Honor; Latinas/os have also served the United States in the
25 Global War on Terrorism, Gulf Wars, and elsewhere; and
26 WHEREAS, In total, 60 Hispanic or Latino veterans have been
27 awarded the Congressional Medal of Honor for service in the United
28 States Armed Forces; the medal is the country's highest award for military
29 valor; and
30 WHEREAS, Today, more than 200,000 Latinas/os are active duty
31 members of the Armed Forces, and more than 1.5 million Latina/o
32 veterans call the United States, including Colorado, home; now, therefore,

1 Be it enacted by the General Assembly of the State of Colorado:
2 SECTION 1. Legislative declaration. (1) The general assembly
3 finds that:
4 (a) The use of second-generation anticoagulant rodenticides,
5 referred to as "SGARs", should be restricted to licensed applicators to
6 safeguard public health, nontarget wildlife and domestic animals, and the
7 environment;
8 (b) Despite prior proposals and actions by the United States
9 environmental protection agency, SGARs remain widely accessible to the
10 general public;
11 (c) Statistics from America's Poison Centers indicate that children
12 5 years old and younger account for 75% of the approximately 3,100
13 annual human exposures to SGARs reported from 2019 to 2023;
14 (d) Preventive, permanent, or nonstructure-associated baiting with
15 SGARs prolongs the environmental presence of toxic chemicals and
16 increases the likelihood of primary and secondary exposure to companion
17 animals and wildlife, including birds of prey and mammalian carnivores;
18 (e) Integrated pest management strategies, which emphasize
19 exclusion, proper sanitation, and targeted removal, minimize harm and
20 constitute a humane, science-driven, and effective approach to rodent
21 control;
22 (f) In conjunction with using integrated pest management
23 strategies, limiting the use of SGARs to circumstances that involve
24 documented and persistent rodent activity, as verified by means such as
-2- 062

1 electronic rodent monitoring and as only a "last resort" could reduce
2 unnecessary exposure risks and cascading household, property, and
3 environmental impacts; and
4 (g) The department of agriculture's broad rule-making authority
5 includes the authority to adopt rules consistent with state and federal law
6 to further reduce the unintended exposure to SGARs, including the
7 adoption of rules that reflect the risk-reduction principles described in this
8 section.
9 (2) The general assembly therefore declares that restricting the use
10 of SGARs, while promoting integrated pest management strategies and
11 encouraging the targeted, structure-associated use of SGARs, will protect
12 Colorado's wildlife, children, and pets; will safeguard human health; and
13 will support humane, science-driven, and effective rodent control.
